Background

India

Two-wheeler vehicle service centre management relied heavily on manual information handoffs between service staff, creating workflow dependencies that result in late service deliveries.

The late service deliveries impacted businesses of the two-wheeler vehicle service centres.

Loss of Daily Service Revenue

Service Workflow

Heuristic Evaluation

Solution overview

SOLUTION 01/03
BEFORE

Unstructured form for vehicle issues documentation (job card)

Poor information architecture and lack of field grouping created a long, unstructured form with no contextual summary of user input.

For example: Frequently edited fields like “Supervisor” required repeated vertical navigation, increasing interaction cost and cognitive load.

AFTER

Multi-step (job card) form with contextual feedback

Implemented a guided navigation with contextual progress indicators, grouping information, and a clear hierarchy.

For example: Frequently edited fields like “Critical Job card” and “Supervisor” were made sticky at the top for constant accessibility.

SOLUTION 02/03
BEFORE

Paper job cards preferred over digital interface

Service technicians relied on printed job cards because they supported quick scanning and task tracking during vehicle repairs.

AFTER

Checklist-like interface for Service Technicians

The redesigned experience converts job cards into a digital checklist optimized for in-workshop use, with touch-friendly interactions and persistent access to supporting tools for efficient task completion and documentation.

SOLUTION 03/03
BEFORE

Manual tracking of service operations

Vehicles’ status across workshops, parking bays, and customer waiting areas was managed manually, relying on service employee to constantly monitor activity on the floor.

For example: There was no clear visibility into how many customers were waiting or which vehicles required immediate attention.

AFTER

Real-time visibility into service operations

A centralized dashboard provides real-time visibility into key stages of vehicle and customer status across the service centre, enabling service teams to respond and manage work effectively.

For example: Critical states like Waiting, Ongoing, Pending, and Billing help teams take timely action.

Understanding the service centre layout & operations

To learn where these fragmentations happen, we studied the spatial layout and workflow structure service centre ecosystem

Most two-wheeler vehicle service centres follow a similar spatial layout organised into four primary operational zones:

  1. 1Vehicle Identification zone
  2. 2Vehicle Service Documentation zone
  3. 3Service Execution zone
  4. 4Vehicle Billing & Delivery zone

Note: For clarity in this case study, the layout is simplified. Some centres also include dedicated areas for inventory storage, vehicle cleaning, and final inspection.

Here’s how a vehicle moves through the service centre from entry to delivery.

1

Vehicle Identification zone

Security guard identifies the vehicle and customer upon entry. Customer waits in the lobby.

2

Vehicle Service Documentation zone

Service staff talks to the customer and documents the vehicle issues in a form called “job card”. Then, assigns the job card to a service staff and estimates the time of service delivery.

3

Service Execution zone

The assigned service staff performs the vehicle service according to the job card.

4

Vehicle Billing & Delivery zone

Once the service is completed, Service staff performs final inspection, proceeds to bill and delivers vehicle to the customer.

We learnt that,

Each vehicle movement between service centre zones triggered manual information handoffs.

These handoffs introduced workflow dependencies which in scale, led to service delays.

Understanding the service staff & vehicle movements

To learn why these fragmentations happen, we studied the service staff's role and workflow in the service centre.

Service staff's roles & responsibilities

Security Guard

  • Identifies & verifies customer and vehicle
  • Creates Token ID for a vehicle

Service Executive

  • Documents vehicle issues and customer concerns
  • Estimates the vehicle time of delivery
  • Assigns Technician and manages ramps allocation

Service Technician

  • Performs the vehicle repair/service
  • Identifies any undiagnosed vehicle issue
  • Undergoes service training

Zoom-in on a vehicle movement & Service staff interaction

We learnt that,

Manual handoffs emerged due to the absence of shared, real-time visibility across roles.

Without a common view of vehicle status and service progress, employees relied on ad-hoc communication to bridge gaps — often leading to delays and inaccuracies.

Manual Handoff

Security Guard verbally relays waiting time and customer load to the Service Executive due to lack of real-time lobby visibility.

Understanding how service staff perceived the tool

We conducted 5 SME interviews, this allowed us to identify critical pain points from the perspective of experienced Hero MotoCorp service professionals.

INSIGHT #1

Post-creation changes to job cards introduced approval bottlenecks.

Because job cards were created with inaccurate information due to lack of visibility, even minor updates required manual approvals from service executives, adding wait time and delaying service progress.

INSIGHT #2

Critical operational visibility was concentrated with Service Executive.

Service Executive - single person behind keeping track of workshop ramp utilisation, customer engagement, approvals and service deliveries.

Evaluating the current service management tool

To effectively frame our design strategy, we evaluated the current state of the tool to understand the usability issues and design limitations.

Gained a pivotal insight

By sharing the findings with stakeholders - product owners, subject matter experts, we learnt an insight that wouldn’t have been discovered otherwise.

INSIGHT #3

Technicians preferred printed job cards for ease of use and manual task management

Technicians found printed job cards more accessible for checking tasks and tracking progress compared to the current job card interface.

Building foundations for the re-design

Before designing the UI, we restructured the operational task flows to reduce manual dependencies and increase system-driven visibility.

Service Executives lacked real-time visibility into customer entry and waiting time

BEFORE

Real-time customer entry signals replaced verbal handoffs

AFTER

Rationale? Real-time token visibility removes manual coordination and gives service executives awareness of customer load, enabling faster prioritisation and smoother intake flow.

Technician progress depended on Service Executive updates to the job card

BEFORE

Technicians log findings directly without waiting for Service executive intervention

AFTER

Rationale? The redesign reduces idle time caused by approvals and improves end-to-end service throughput during peak operations.

Translating workflows into interfaces

As we restructured service workflows, we learnt that each role operates in a distinct physical environment (zones) with its own limitations influencing how they interact with the system.

Design interfaces around operational behaviours

Security Guard
Mobile-first
Supports fast scanning, mobility, and one-handed interactions.
Service Executive
Tablet-first
Enables management of multiple vehicles, approvals, and real-time decisions simultaneously.
Service Technician
Tablet-first
Enables detailed interaction with job cards, parts, and inspection workflows during service.

Iteration 1
Prioritising Critical Delay Touch points

We prioritized Tokens and Approvals over Billing and Inspections as these created the operational bottlenecks.

If the blockers like waiting tokens or job card change approvals aren't cleared first, the entire service pipeline stalls.

Findings

The aha moment! ⤵

Dashboard lacked adaptive guidance across different operational states. During low-traffic periods (no new tokens), the interface lost relevance.

High-volume conditions exposed interaction cost issues. Retrieval of online and walk-in tokens became time-intensive.

Priority in service operations is time- and context-driven, not status-driven. A job card's urgency may shift depending on time of day, customer presence, and workload.

Iterations

Iteration 1

Followed a visibility-first approach but lacked contextual relevance.

Final iteration

Context-driven operational workspace that reduces decision friction.

Similarly, we validated the other concepts through cross-functional reviews with Subject Matter Experts, Product, and Engineering teams. These sessions helped ground the design in real operational workflows.

Final designs & feature highlights

Dashboard of customer and vehicles’ status in the vehicle service centre.

Four important stages Waiting, Ongoing, Pending, Billing jobs are the key metrics to keep track of operations in vehicle centre.

Push notification and critical alerts enable prioritisation and effective management.

Alerts about delays, prolonged customer waiting, ready to bill job cards allow executives to act proactively rather than reactively.

Multi-step form with contextual feedback

A guided navigation with contextual progress indicators, grouping information, and a clear hierarchy.

Checklist-like interface for Service Technicians

Updated job-card layout for service technicians use with checklist UI, touch-friendly interactions and help & documentation features.

Real-time ramp planning

Track ramp occupancy and move job cards in real time to balance workload, minimise idle time, and maintain steady service flow.
