Background

Carnegie Mellon University offers a massive catalog of 8,000+ courses across undergraduate and graduate programs.
This abundance while it is empowering, it also creates significant cognitive overload during course selection. With limited guidance to navigate the course selection process, students fall into the trap of 'unawareness', missing the very opportunities they came here to find.

Course Selection Process

The current tool allows students to narrow their course options using filters such as credits, course duration, keywords.

While these filters help with logistics, it doesn’t really help the students with decision-making.

Identified Problem

Through interviews with students, program advisors, and educators, we explored how course decisions are actually made and where the friction lies.

1
Decision-making is socially distributed

Course selection is heavily influenced by student's network. The trust flows through peers (seniors, alumni) who’ve had taken the course. Hence, students without strong networks are disproportionately disadvantaged.

2
Students seek trajectory and not just enrolment.

We learnt from program advisors that students make decisions following two behavioural models - Exploratory and Strategic. Exploratory students experiment with electives to discover interests. Strategic students intentionally target towards a skill, role or industry.

3
Academic advisors capacity is structurally limited.

Academic or program advisors manage about 300 students. This limits their bandwidth to provide personalized trajectory planning to students at scale.

Problem Statement

How might we design an AI course recommendation system that supports both exploratory and trajectory-driven students while earning trust, promoting equity, and delivering reliable guidance?

Trust

Students rely on peers and alumni recommendations because they look for credibility and relevance.

FEATURE Explainable recommendations
Equity

The recommendations must not perpetuate the existing bias by just promoting the most-enrolled courses.

FEATURE Algorithm-level equity
Reliability

The recommendations should reflect students' intent and real constraints (prerequisites, workload, timing).

FEATURE Intent + Constraints‑aware logic

Introducing Course Compass

The experience centers on a short diagnostic flow and a ranked, explainable recommendation list.
